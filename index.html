The signature

Four things every frame gets right

Nothing here is a mood. Each one is a decision a photographer can either make or fail to make on the day.

Light

One source, hard edge. Direct sun or a bare window. The shadow is a subject, not a by-product, and it is never filled. No strobe, no softbox, no bounce.

Grade

Warm, never orange. Highlights hold rather than clip, oil reads gold, whites stay off-white. Saturation sits slightly under true.

Ground

Warm neutral paper, concrete, a painted wall. Flat surfaces only. The ground shifts tone with the light rather than being lit separately.

Subject

The oil, the hands holding it, the people around the table. Product is held, not styled. Props are the tools of the ritual only.

01 · THE PACK, LIT ONCE

One window, one hard-edged shadow, one bottle. The ground is a warm neutral that shifts with the light. Never a colored sweep, never a second light brought in to fill the shadow.

02 · THE BOTTLE IN HAND

Product held, not styled. A hand and a face give the bottle its scale. Lens flare and a little softness are allowed here; they read as a real room rather than a set.

03 · THE DAILY RITUAL

The mini bottles carry the direct ritual. Cupped hands, oil backlit to gold, the gesture of offering. Nobody is shown drinking on camera.

04 · THE SET, REPEATED

Repetition as the subject. Ranked rows in low sun, or scattered flat on a neutral ground. Both readings are approved. Hold one grade across a single campaign rather than mixing them.

05 · THE TABLE

The lineup on a working surface with the person behind it. Props are the tools of the ritual only: pourer, sip glass, minis. Nothing decorative enters the frame.

06 · PEOPLE

DZ and the people around the brand, shot in the same light as the pack. Warm, direct, unstyled. These frames belong on About and in founder-voice placements, not on a PDP.

The commission

Eight shoots that would close the library

Ordered by what unblocks the most surfaces. Each holds the same light rule as the March shoot: one source, hard edge, no fill.

Cooking, mid-prep

Priority 1 · Blocks the PDP and all COOK marketing

A real kitchen, a hot pan, ingredients partway through. The pour is the action — oil leaving the bottle and hitting the pan or the board.

  • Pour into a hot pan, oil catching the light as it falls
  • Hand drizzling over a finished dish, plate half in frame
  • The 400ml standing on a working counter beside prep, not styled
  • Ingredients mid-prep with the bottle in the background, out of focus

The shared table, with people

Priority 1 · The table has food now, but no group around it

More than one person, food actually being eaten, the bottle present rather than featured. This is the Culinary Ritual as a social act.

  • Four or more people at a table mid-meal, bottle within reach
  • Plates being passed, hands crossing the frame
  • Someone finishing a dish at the table with the pourer
  • The table after — empty plates, the bottle still standing

A wider cast

Priority 1 · Every human frame is one person

One man in his thirties carries nearly every frame with a person in it. For a brand whose line is that wellness is not one size fits all, the casting currently says the opposite.

  • Women, and a range of ages including 50-plus
  • A family or household, not only individuals
  • Someone cooking who reads as a home cook rather than a model
  • Hands of different ages holding the same mini

The morning, in a real room

Priority 2 · The Direct Ritual has no setting

Current ritual frames are hands presenting minis against a seamless. The ritual is a moment in a day, and no frame establishes that.

  • Early light through a kitchen window, mini on the counter
  • The Sip Glass being filled, not held
  • The bottle where it lives — a shelf, a counter, beside the kettle
  • Wider room frames giving the ritual an address

Provenance

Priority 2 · Dropped in the six-subject rewrite

The earlier direction carried ingredient integrity — grove, fruit, mill. The rewrite lost it, and a certified-organic single-origin claim needs to be seen.

  • The grove at ground level, not only the aerial
  • Fruit on the branch and in the bin at harvest
  • The mill running, and oil at first press
  • Hands of the grower, shot as the people frames are shot

The 1 gallon and the full range at scale

Priority 2 · Foodservice has zero frames

The style set is minis-heavy. The gallon is a real SKU with no photography, and nothing shows the sizes together.

  • The 1 gallon in a commercial kitchen, in use
  • Gallon, 400ml and mini in one frame for scale
  • The three-part practice as one flat-lay set
  • Case and shipper for wholesale and trade decks

Shelf and retail environment

Priority 3 · Retail exists only as a placeholder

Needed for trade decks, retailer pitches and the 3-second shelf test, which the system asks vendors to run without giving them a reference.

  • The pack on shelf beside real competitors
  • A shopper reaching for the bottle
  • Shelf talker and end cap in situ
  • The pack at 100px, as proof of the thumbnail test

Crops and vertical motion

Priority 3 · Format, not subject

Everything in the library ships 3:2 or 2:3 native. The system asks vendors for 4:5, 1:1, 16:9 and 9:16, and none are delivered.

  • Deliver all four crops per approved frame, at 2× retina
  • Shoot loose enough that 9:16 and 1:1 survive the crop
  • Vertical-native frames for Stories and Reels covers
  • 16:9 lifestyle bands with room for a scrim and headline

Two things to decide before booking

The March set is one shoot in one light. Every frame reads as the same day, which is why the library feels consistent and also why it feels narrow. A second shoot in a different season would test whether the light rule is a style or an accident.

The ritual frames stop short of consumption on purpose — nobody is shown drinking. That rule is doing compliance work as much as aesthetic work, and it is worth confirming it is deliberate before a photographer inherits it.

Voice, copy and claim compliance

Calm authority, editorial restraint, declarative. The brand states facts and lets them stand. Every surface obeys the same claim list: packaging, PDP, ads, email, influencer briefs. A flagged term is a legal problem, not a copy edit.

The full document

This page is the short form. The Tone of Voice document carries the long-form guidance — audience, register, worked examples and the reasoning behind the claim list.

Open the Tone of Voice doc
Casing

Sentence case for headlines and body. ALL CAPS for section labels, button labels and pack display type only, never for a sentence. Product names are always uppercase: COOK, HEAL, HEAL+.

Person

Second person for instruction: "Take a tablespoon in the morning." First person plural for the brand's own actions: "We test every harvest." The founder's "I" appears only on the About page — David Zimmerman, who signs founder emails DZ.

Punctuation

No exclamation marks anywhere. Em dashes sparingly. Numbers are numerals when they are measurements: 311, 400ml, 24%.

Marks

Emoji never — not in UI, not in email, not in social. Unicode only as functional marks: → in category links, · as a separator, − in savings lines, ★ in ratings.
